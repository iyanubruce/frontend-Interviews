# Evaluation Criteria for Front End System Design Interviews

> Source: [evaluation-axes](https://www.greatfrontend.com/front-end-system-design-playbook/evaluation-axes)

During interviews, interviewers look out for signals displayed by candidates before making an overall hiring and leveling recommendation. The more desired behaviors the candidate displays, the higher the likelihood the interviewer will recommend a "Hire" decision. The more detailed and mature the answers are, the higher the leveling recommendation.

This section lists some of the behaviors that candidates should display. Bear them in mind as you are answering the system design question and demonstrate them confidently during the interview.

> **Interviewers score observed signals, not unspoken reasoning**
>
> If you thought about a tradeoff but never verbalized it, it did not happen as far as the evaluation is concerned. Make every decision and consideration visible through your narration and diagrams.

## Problem exploration

- Demonstrated thorough understanding of the problem.
- Explored the requirements sufficiently by asking relevant clarifying questions to minimize ambiguities.
- Gathered functional and non-functional requirements of the problem.
- Defined the scope of the problem.
- Identified the important aspects of the problem to focus on and address.

**Relevant framework sections:** Requirements exploration

## Architecture

- Developed an architecture that solved the entire problem sufficiently.
- Broke down the problem into smaller independent parts of suitable granularity.
- Identified components of the system and defined their responsibilities clearly.
- Articulated how these components will work together and defined/described the API between these components.
- Developed an architecture that can be put into practice.
- Developed an architecture with scalability and reusability in mind, one that can be extended to support future requirements.

**Relevant framework sections:** Architecture/High-level design, Data model, Interface definition

## Technical proficiency

- Demonstrated technical knowledge and proficiency of front end fundamentals, common technologies and APIs.
- Dove into specific front end domain areas where relevant to the problem.
- Identified areas which need to be paid special attention to and addressed them by proposing solutions and analyzing their tradeoffs.

_Front end domain areas include Performance, Networking, HTML/CSS, Accessibility, Internationalization, Security, Scalability, etc._

**Relevant framework sections:** Architecture / High-level design, Optimizations and deep dive

## Exploration and tradeoffs

- Offered various possible solutions to the problems at hand and explained the pros and cons of each solution.
  - The "problem" here doesn't necessarily refer to the given system design question.
  - While solving the given question, there would be smaller problems to solve/questions to answer and each small problem/question can have various solutions and choices to make.
- Explained the suitability of the solutions given the context and requirements and provided recommendations for the context of the question.
  - Do not insist there is only one possible solution. Good questions usually have a few possible solutions where the suitability of each depends on the context.
  - Even if the other solutions are clearly and obviously bad, do still mention them and briefly explain why they are bad.

> **Show the alternatives you rejected, not just the one you picked**
>
> Naming two or three options and articulating why the chosen one fits the context is a stronger leveling signal than arriving at the same final answer silently. Dismissing obvious non-starters out loud still counts as tradeoff reasoning.

**Relevant framework sections:** Requirements exploration, Data model, Interface definition, Optimizations and deep dive

## Product and UX sense

- Proposed a solution that accounts for loading, failures, different devices, and keyboard use.
- Considered user experience when answering: loading states, performance (perceived or actual), mobile friendliness, keyboard friendliness, etc.
- Considered error cases and suggested ways to handle them.

**Relevant framework sections:** Optimizations and deep dive

## Communication and collaboration

- Conveyed their thoughts and ideas clearly and concisely.
- Explained complex concepts with ease.
- Engaged the interviewer during the session, asked good questions and sought opinions where relevant.
- Was open to feedback from the interviewer and incorporated the feedback to refine their solutions.

**Relevant framework sections:** Architecture / High-level design, Data model, Interface definition, Optimizations and deep dive

## Summary

Here's a table summarizing how the evaluation axes can be mapped to the various sections of the **RADIO framework**: Requirements Exploration, Architecture/High-level Design, Data Model, Interface Definition, Optimizations and Deep Dive.

| Axis                            | R   | A   | D   | I   | O   |
| ------------------------------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem exploration             | ✅  | \-  | \-  | \-  | \-  |
| Architecture                    | \-  | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  | \-  |
| Technical proficiency           | \-  | ✅  | \-  | \-  | ✅  |
| Exploration and tradeoffs       | \-  | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  |
| Product and UX sense            | \-  | \-  | \-  | \-  | ✅  |
| Communication and collaboration | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  | ✅  |

## Exercises

### Check your understanding 1

Which evaluation-axis and RADIO-phase mappings are correct? Select all that apply.

- [x] Problem exploration is primarily demonstrated during Requirements exploration.
  > This mapping fits because clarifying ambiguity, defining scope, and gathering requirements are the signals on that axis.
- [x] Architecture signals appear across Architecture, Data model, and Interface definition.
  > These phases reveal the system's parts, responsibilities, data ownership, and concrete communication contracts.
- [ ] Product and UX sense is demonstrated only while listing data fields.
  > This is incorrect because product and UX signals are most visible in optimizations and deep dives such as loading, errors, and accessibility.
- [ ] Communication and collaboration matter only during the first requirements questions.
  > This is incorrect because clear narration, feedback, and interviewer engagement are evaluated throughout all RADIO phases.

### Check your understanding 2

Which response best explains the choice between alternative designs?

- [x] Describe viable alternatives, compare their costs and benefits, then recommend one based on the agreed requirements.
  > This is strongest because it makes both the decision process and contextual suitability observable to the interviewer.
- [ ] Declare one technology universally best and decline to discuss alternatives.
  > This is weak because system design choices depend on context and usually have meaningful tradeoffs.
- [ ] Name several approaches without explaining differences or choosing one.
  > This is incomplete because an unranked list does not show evaluation or a context-based recommendation.
- [ ] Choose an appropriate approach internally and draw only the final box.
  > This hides the signal because interviewers cannot score reasoning that the candidate never communicates.

### Check your understanding 3

Which behaviors make product sense and collaboration visible during a system design interview? Select all that apply.

- [x] Address loading, error, mobile, performance, and keyboard experiences where they affect the product.
  > This shows consideration for users because the design accounts for real conditions beyond the happy path.
- [x] Narrate decisions, ask focused questions, seek the interviewer's view, and incorporate relevant feedback.
  > This demonstrates collaboration because the interview becomes a shared design exercise rather than a monologue.
- [ ] Remain silent while refining the design and reveal only the final diagram.
  > This hides communication and decision signals because unspoken reasoning cannot be evaluated.
- [ ] Discuss only the successful desktop flow because error and accessibility cases are implementation details.
  > This weakens product sense because robust front end design includes failure, device, and accessibility considerations.
